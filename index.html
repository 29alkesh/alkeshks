Alkesh K. Srivastava

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Temple Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (TRAIL) at Temple University, advised by Dr. Philip Dames. My research focuses on multi-robot coordination, planning, and decision-making under uncertainty, with an emphasis on developing algorithms that enable robot teams to cooperatively transport resources and operate effectively in constrained and uncertain environments. My work spans material transport, information gathering and transport, and language- and vision-guided robotic decision-making, integrating mathematical modeling, optimization, probabilistic reasoning, and foundation models for autonomous robotic systems.

I am also a Visiting Ph.D. Student at the General Robotics, Automation, Sensing & Perception (GRASP) Laboratory at the University of Pennsylvania, hosted by Dr. Vijay Kumar, and a Visiting Researcher at the DEVCOM U.S. Army Research Laboratory (ARL), working with Carlos P. Nieto-Granda. Previously, I held an ORAU Journeyman Fellowship at ARL, where I conducted research on distributed autonomy and multi-robot decision-making in challenging environments.

Before joining Temple University, I earned a Master of Engineering degree in Robotics from the University of Maryland, College Park, where I conducted research at the Motion and Teaming Laboratory under the supervision of Dr. Michael Otte. My research involved probabilistic graphical models, information theory, and multi-robot information gathering in communication-denied environments. I received my Bachelor of Technology degree in Electrical Engineering from Rajasthan Technical University.

I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Summer 2027 and am interested in postdoctoral research opportunities in robotics, autonomous systems, and multi-agent intelligence.

Research Interests

My research lies at the intersection of multi-robot systems, autonomous planning, optimization, and decision-making under uncertainty. I am particularly interested in understanding how teams of autonomous robots can coordinate to efficiently acquire, transport, and utilize resources in complex environments. My work combines theoretical modeling, algorithm design, probabilistic inference, and learning-based approaches to develop scalable and adaptive robotic systems.

My current research spans three interconnected directions:

  • Multi-Robot Coordination and Resource Transport: Developing cooperative planning algorithms for resource transport, including relay-based pickup and delivery, adaptive transport structures, task allocation, and coordination under resource and environmental constraints.
  • Information Gathering and Decision-Making Under Uncertainty: Investigating distributed sensing, Bayesian estimation, information-theoretic planning, behavioral valuation, and information transport in multi-robot systems, particularly in hazardous and communication-constrained environments.
  • Vision-Language and Large Language Models for Robotics: Exploring the integration of VLMs and LLMs into autonomous robotic systems for natural-language task understanding, semantic reasoning, target search, spatial uncertainty estimation, and language-guided multi-robot planning.

My broader goal is to bridge theoretical foundations with practical robotic autonomy, enabling intelligent robot teams to operate reliably in real-world applications such as search and rescue, distributed exploration, infrastructure monitoring, and autonomous logistics.
