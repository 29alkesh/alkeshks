About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the Temple Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (TRAIL) at Temple University, advised by Prof. Philip Dames. My research focuses on multi-robot coordination, planning, and decision-making under uncertainty, particularly how robotic teams can cooperatively transport resources in constrained environments. I study material transport, information gathering and transport, and language- and vision-guided robotic decision-making.

I am a Visiting Ph.D. Student at the GRASP Laboratory at the University of Pennsylvania, hosted by Prof. Vijay Kumar, and a Visiting Researcher at the DEVCOM U.S. Army Research Laboratory, working with Carlos P. Nieto-Granda. Previously, I was an ORAU Journeyman Fellow at ARL.

Prior to Temple, I received an M.Eng. in Robotics from the University of Maryland, College Park, where I worked with Prof. Michael Otte at the Motion and Teaming Laboratory. I earned my B.Tech. in Electrical Engineering from Rajasthan Technical University.

Open to postdoctoral opportunities for 2027 I expect to complete my Ph.D. in Summer 2027.

Research Interests

My research combines mathematical modeling, algorithm design, probabilistic inference, and foundation models to make autonomous robot teams more adaptive and robust.

Multi-Robot Coordination & Resource Transport

Relay-based pickup and delivery, adaptive transport structures, task allocation, and coordinated logistics under resource constraints.

Information Gathering & Decision-Making

Distributed sensing, Bayesian estimation, information-theoretic planning, and behavioral valuation in hazardous or communication-denied environments.

VLMs & LLMs for Robotics

Natural-language task understanding, spatial-semantic reasoning, target search, and language-guided multi-robot planning.
